Reading this table:
  * 'scoreable' counts runs with >=5 non-tied groups. A run below that
    is reported by _detection_stats() as NaN, not as a low score, and
    is excluded rather than averaged in.
  * 'tie-excluded' is the share of candidate groups dropped because the
    top-two replicas were within the run's own resolution floor. A high
    share is #52's outcome (ii): the data cannot decide the question.
  * The baseline row is the control. If baseline and chaos score alike,
    the detector is detecting nothing -- a healthy cluster scored 0.6409
    against a 0.333 chance line on nano-db purely from ties.
